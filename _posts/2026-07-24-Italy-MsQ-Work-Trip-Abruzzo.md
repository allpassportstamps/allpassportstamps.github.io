I am here to accompany Ms. Q this week on a business trip, and I am so grateful to my employer, R, who allowed me to be flexible for part of the week and to have Thursday and Friday for an extended weekend. 

* Mainly explored the region of Abruzzo in central Italy and Rome
* Many folks from the US are descendants of Southern Italians, and it's strikingly clear where the US got its love for pizza and Italian food, as well as the cultural influences.
* Folks here have dinner quite late. A lot of restaurants told us that they open at 7:30pm, and many folks would run errands before going to dinner and then go to bed right after eating.

This trip had some wonderful culture shocks that I did not expect, and I loved every minute of exploring the intricacies of the culture that I thought I knew.

### Day 1: Settling in Avezzano

Ms. Q and I arrived on a Monday afternoon. We drove for about an hour and a half to the town of Avezzano. 

![hotel_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hotel_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![hotel_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hotel_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*The view outside our hotel balcony: the back of a farm at the foot of a mountain*

Our rental car was a BYD hybrid, and it really took us a while to get familiar with the basics, like how to switch from EV to HEV modes. 

The evening was a bit rough for me with R, but I managed to survive. 

### Day 2: Celano and Tagliacozzo Towns

I dropped off Ms. Q at her work on the outskirts of town and got to explore the surrounding towns a bit.

My first attraction was the town of Celano, which featured Piccolomini Castle at its heart. 

![piccolomini_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/piccolomini_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}![piccolomini_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/piccolomini_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Piccolomini Castle was built in the 1400s and awarded to a noble. The castle was abandoned before eventually becoming a museum.

The castle museum focused on a few stories:
* Lake Fucino was once a big part of the region, but it often flooded. Emperor Claudius tried to drain it, but the lake was not drained until the 1870s, when it became very flat farmland. Many Roman ruins were found over time as part of the excavation.
* Stories of the Piccolomini family, who had stewardship of the castle.
* The big 1915 earthquake in this region, which devastated big towns like L'Aquila and damaged the castle as well.

![celano_view]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/celano_view.jpeg){:width="50%"}

After walking around the town and breathing in the mountain air, I went to my next stop, Tagliacozzo.

![tagliacozzo_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/tagliacozzo_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![tagliacozzo_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/tagliacozzo_2.jpeg){:width="28%"}

Tagliacozzo has a stunning backdrop of mountain houses. I walked through the piazza and along the famous mountain river walk. This is a town where folks from Rome escape to during the hot summer months. 

I also had time to walk around Avezzano. At around 1pm on a weekday, the town square and the shops were mostly empty, except for caffè and gelato places where some folks gathered.

> I had a haircut in the Arab community part of the town. It's becoming a recurring pattern: I have had haircuts so far in quite a few places, like Krakow, Cotonou, Accra, and now Avezzano.

That evening, I picked up Ms. Q, and we enjoyed a great square pizza takeout. 

> Ms. Q loved her coworkers and had gotten quite a few wonderful recommendations.

![pizza]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/pizza.jpeg){:width="50%"}

That evening, I had a meeting until 12:30am, then had another meeting from 3am until 4:15am. Was I crazy?

### Day 3: Fever and Sulmona

Having had plenty of symptoms the previous day, I was officially pretty feverish. I dropped off Ms. Q at work and caught up on some sleep. At around 2:30pm, I decided that I wasn't going to do absolutely nothing that day. 

![sulmona_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/sulmona_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![sulmona_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/sulmona_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

The town of Sulmona is about an hour away; the town specializes in confetti milk chocolates called Confetti Pelino. The souvenir shops had arranged these tiny chocolates into beautiful flowers and cute items. The confetti frog, flower bouquet, and ladybugs were exquisite.

> I ordered a caffè latte since I knew the famous rule that cappuccinos can't be ordered after lunchtime. So I guess I got the next best thing.

![sulmona_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/sulmona_3.jpeg){:width="28%"}![sulmona_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/sulmona_4.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*Right: bicyclist drinking from a water fountain with sculptural engravings*

The cafe I sat at was run by an old man. The coffee was great and, like in most of Italy, cost around 1.3 euros, a very cheap figure compared with US prices. 

I somehow survived my meetings. Ms. Q and I ate at a nearby restaurant that her coworker recommended. The spaghetti and lamb were great. 

![dinner_fever]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/dinner_fever.jpeg){:width="28%"}

We learned that Italian dinners always have antipasti (appetizers), primo (carbs), and secondo (meats). We thought that each person was supposed to get a first and a second, and we definitely could not finish them. 

Feeling quite feverish, I ended my evening early at R and tried to recover from the fever. 

### Day 4: Tivoli Trip: Villa d'Este and Villa Adriana (Hadrian)

Sweating profusely and with my fever somewhat broken in the morning, I drove an hour to Tivoli, the escape from Rome. 

![este_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/este_1.jpeg){:width="28%"}![este_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/este_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Villa d'Este is a huge Renaissance mansion and garden, built by the d'Este family for a powerful cardinal who was related to the pope.  

I descended two levels and saw the lavish rooms before entering the garden with its very impressive array of fountains.

![este_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/este_3.jpeg){:width="50%"}![este_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/este_4.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*Left: many fish in the pond, overlooking the plains*

The fountains were powered by an aqueduct supplied by a nearby river... and made the very impressive gardens cool and a refuge in the blazing Mediterranean summer.

My next stop was Hadrian's Villa (Villa Adriana). This was Hadrian's escape from Rome, many times the size of Pompeii.

![hadrian_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hadrian_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![hadrian_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hadrian_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*Left: The unique Maritime Theater with an aquatic backdrop*

> Hadrian, one of the Five Good Emperors, was famous for being a builder who secured Trajan's expansions and a prolific traveler to the provinces of Rome.

I touched stones and pillars that have been around for 1,900 years. These ruins were powerful testaments to the height of the Roman Empire, which ruled the ancient Mediterranean. 

![hadrian_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hadrian_3.jpeg){:width="50%"}![hadrian_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/hadrian_4.jpeg){:width="50%"}

The men's bathhouses were quite impressive. There were many elements of Hadrian's travels, like the pond named after a section of the Nile.

![gelato]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/gelato.jpeg){:width="50%"}![family_res]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/family_res.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Ms. Q and I tried to get dinner in downtown Avezzano after work before my 8pm meeting, but no restaurant was open before 7. We got gelato before going to a family seafood restaurant at around 9pm. We got to witness the Italian family-style restaurant running well past 10pm, full of families gathering and friends catching a smoke, giving us a glimpse into daily central Italian life.

### Day 5: Scanno Lake and Town, before Rome

By Friday, my fever was finally gone, and I had no meetings! After dropping off Ms. Q, I went to Scanno. There were quite a few dramatic mountain ranges, and I got to see a shepherd guiding his flock of sheep with his dogs.

![scanno_lake_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_lake_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![scanno_lake_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_lake_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Lake Scanno is not a big lake; maybe one can drive the perimeter in around 15 minutes. It is shaped like a heart, visible from a popular one-hour hiking spot, which I unfortunately did not have the time or stamina for.

![scanno_lake_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_lake_3.jpeg){:width="50%"}![scanno_lake_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_lake_4.jpeg){:width="28%"}

After filling up my water bottle using the "Big Nose", I was greeted by views of Scanno village.

![scanno_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![scanno_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*Right: Goat cheese shop*

The village had narrow streets and alleyways. Anywhere you went, you could steal glances at the view of the mountains. The town is famous for its goats; quite a few souvenirs were dedicated to the goat skewers.

![scanno_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_3.jpeg){:width="50%"}![scanno_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/scanno_4.jpeg){:width="28%"}

I stopped for some pasta (I have literally been trying a new type of pasta every day here in Italy!!) before Scanno decided to give me a parking ticket at a place where someone else had also parked earlier...

### Rome, at last!

Ms. Q got off work, and we took the journey back to Rome! After about 1.5 hours on the bus and 30 minutes on the subway, we officially started our weekend! 

We saw many tourist attractions: St. Angelo Bridge, Piazza Navona, the Pantheon, and Hadrian's Temple, and then had dinner.

![st_angelo]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/st_angelo.jpeg){:width="50%"}![dinner]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/dinner.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Rome in August, of course, was full of tourists. However, that did not change the fact that this is Rome, with the sheer density of historical monuments sprawling everywhere we went. 

![trevi]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/trevi.jpeg){:width="50%"}

We stayed past 10pm and made a wish at the Trevi Fountain!

### Day 6: Vatican and Colosseum

![vatican_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/vatican_1.jpeg){:width="28%"}

Saturday was our marathon day in the August sun. We started the day with a bus tour of Vatican City. 

The bus had an audio guide about how Vatican City works, the relics from the 16th century, the popes who have added personal touches, the rail stations, and more. 

![vatican_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/vatican_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}![vatican_3]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/vatican_3.jpeg){:width="50%"}


After the tour, we began feasting our eyes on the art. 

![msq_and_i]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/msq_and_i.jpeg){:width="40%"}

We made our way from the statues to the maps, the stuccos, the Renaissance paintings, and modern art (our least favorite!).

![vatican_4]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/vatican_4.jpeg){:width="50%"}![vatican_5]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/vatican_5.jpeg){:width="50%"}
*Left: Raphael in his own painting; Right: Sistine Chapel*

After finishing at the Sistine Chapel, Ms. Q and I visited St. Peter's Basilica next. 

![st_peter_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/st_peter_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![st_peter_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/st_peter_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

And man, it is full of luxurious statues and artwork, with so many small churches inside the church. Having been to so many churches, I found this one really the grandest! 

![swiss]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/swiss.jpeg){:width="50%"}

As we exited, we caught a glimpse of the Swiss Guard, who have been protecting the pope for five centuries! 

![roman_forum_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/roman_forum_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![roman_forum_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/roman_forum_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Having only the weekend in Rome, our next stop was the Roman Forum. It was excavated in the 1800s, uncovering all the old Roman temples and ruins as the city floor had risen over time.

We saw the gigantic columns, the gates, and the temples for the gods! 

![colosseum_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/colosseum_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![colosseum_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/colosseum_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Of course, our next stop was the Colosseum. Having visited the one in El Jem, Tunisia, I found that the real Colosseum was slightly bigger! 

Our tickets only allowed us to visit the first two floors. It was still very fascinating to learn about the engineering of the stadium, the subfloor where gladiators and animals were positioned, and how everything ran!

![pantheon_1]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/pantheon_1.jpeg){:width="50%"}![pantheon_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/pantheon_2.jpeg){:width="28%"}

Our last stop was the Pantheon. We saw the dome that Hadrian repaired, which still stands after 1,900 years. We admired the circular opening in the roof and the tiny drainage holes in the marble floors, as well as Raphael's tomb. 

![dinner_2]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/dinner_2.jpeg){:width="50%"}

After an exhausting day, Ms. Q and I rested at the hotel restaurant. I attended my MBA class online (about mergers and acquisitions), and Ms. Q planned her next week in Italy, as I was leaving the next day.

### Day 7: Ciao Rome!

![coffee]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/coffee.jpeg){:width="50%"}![market]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/market.jpeg){:width="50%"}

Ms. Q and I explored Rome for around 1.5 hours. We went to the Mouth of Truth, where Ms. Q replayed her movie moment. We walked around Tiber Island, a tiny island about a block wide, and toured the flower market, which is mostly a souvenir market now.

> Standing at a cafe is a coffee culture custom for a quick boost of energy without paying seating fees

![bus]({{site.baseurl}}/assets/img/italy/bus.jpeg){:width="33%"}

After checking out of the hotel, Ms. Q went to Tivoli by herself, and I went to the airport. As a last surprise, the train to the airport was closed because a train had caught fire two days earlier! 

Italy has so many amazing cities -- I hope to be back to visit soon!
